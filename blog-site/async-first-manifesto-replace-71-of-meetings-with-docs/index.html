Async-First Manifesto: Replace 71% of Meetings with Docs

📅 May 23, 2026 ✦ AgendaBurn Editorial

The Async-First Manifesto: A 7-Step Guide to Replacing 71% of Your Meetings with Documents

Here is the uncomfortable truth your calendar refuses to say out loud: 71% of senior managers admit that most meetings are unproductive and inefficient, according to Harvard Business Review. US companies collectively burn $37 billion per year on unnecessary meetings — and the average employee loses 31 hours every single month sitting in them.

That is not a scheduling problem. That is a cultural default — the reflex to "jump on a quick call" rather than write a clear document. The Async-First Manifesto challenges that reflex head-on. This guide gives you a concrete, 7-step system to identify which meetings can be killed, what to replace them with, and how to build a documentation culture that actually sticks.

Definition — Async-First Culture: An async-first workplace is one where the default mode of communication is written, recorded, or documented — and synchronous meetings are reserved only for decisions that genuinely require real-time human interaction, such as conflict resolution, live brainstorming, or sensitive personnel conversations.

Why Most Meetings Should Never Have Been Scheduled

Before building a replacement system, it helps to understand why meetings proliferate in the first place. Meetings feel like work. They create an illusion of progress without requiring the cognitive effort of writing clearly. When something is unclear, it is faster to talk than to think — but talking does not create a searchable, shareable, scalable artefact. A document does.

A single 10-person meeting with senior staff costs between $1,500 and $3,000 per hour when you factor in loaded salaries. Multiply that across weekly stand-ups, cross-functional syncs, and "alignment calls," and you can see why meeting costs spiral invisibly. Companies that start tracking meeting costs reduce meeting time by 30% within three months. Visibility creates accountability. But visibility alone is not enough — you need a replacement system.

The 7-Step Async-First Framework

Follow these steps in order. Each builds on the last. Skipping to step five without completing step two is how async initiatives collapse within a fortnight.

  1. Conduct a Meeting Audit
    Export your last 30 days of calendar data. For every recurring meeting, ask one question: "Would anything break if this meeting became a weekly written update instead?" Flag every meeting where the honest answer is no. Studies consistently show that status updates, progress reports, and informational broadcasts account for the majority of meeting volume — and all three translate directly to documents.
  2. Define Your Meeting-Worthy Criteria
    Create a written policy — not a Slack message, an actual document — that defines exactly when a meeting is justified. A useful starting framework: a meeting is warranted only when (a) a decision requires live negotiation, (b) emotional context matters, or (c) the topic demands simultaneous creative input from multiple people. Everything else defaults to async.
  3. Build a Document-First Template Library
    The reason people default to meetings is friction: writing feels hard, and scheduling a call feels easy. Eliminate that friction by creating reusable templates for the most common meeting replacements. At minimum, build templates for: project status updates, decision briefs, proposal reviews, onboarding walkthroughs, and retrospectives.
  4. Establish Response Windows, Not Real-Time Expectations
    Async breaks down when people treat a Slack message like a meeting — expecting instant replies. Define explicit response windows across your team. A sensible baseline: routine async messages receive a response within four hours during working hours; decision-blocking questions within 24 hours; anything urgent can escalate to a synchronous call with a written brief attached.
  5. Replace Your Standing Meetings One at a Time
    Do not attempt a full async migration in a single week. Pick your lowest-value recurring meeting — usually a weekly status sync — and replace it with a structured written update for four weeks. Gather feedback. Iterate on the template. Then move to the next candidate. Incremental replacement builds trust and surfaces edge cases before they become crises.
  6. Create a Decision Log and Make It the Source of Truth
    One of the legitimate reasons people cling to meetings is accountability — decisions made verbally disappear. Solve this by maintaining a shared Decision Log: a single document (or Notion page, or Confluence entry) where every significant decision is recorded with context, the decision made, who owns it, and the date. When decisions live in writing, the need for "follow-up calls" evaporates.
  7. Measure, Reward, and Reinforce Async Behaviour
    What gets measured gets managed. Track the number of meetings per week, average meeting duration, and the ratio of meetings to documents produced. Celebrate publicly when a team resolves a complex issue asynchronously. Create a lightweight "async kudos" channel. Cultural change requires positive reinforcement, not just policy enforcement.

What to Do with the Meetings That Remain

Going async-first does not mean going meeting-free. Some meetings genuinely deserve to exist. The goal is to make the meetings that remain excellent, not merely tolerated. Here is how to protect the quality of your necessary synchronous time:

  • No meeting without a written pre-read. Every meeting should have an agenda document distributed at least 24 hours in advance. If the organiser cannot write a pre-read, the meeting probably should not happen.
  • Default to 25 or 50 minutes, never 30 or 60. The buffer forces people to end early and prevents back-to-back meeting marathons.
  • Assign a rotating decision-logger. One person's sole job is to capture decisions and actions in writing during the meeting. This document is shared within 30 minutes of the meeting ending.
  • End every meeting with one question: "Could this have been a document?" Over time, honest answers to that question reshape how people think about scheduling entirely.
  • Use AI meeting summaries for the meetings you cannot eliminate, so the output is always searchable and actionable rather than buried in someone's memory.

The Real Cost of Not Going Async

Async-first is not about making people work harder or eliminating human connection. It is about respecting the scarcest resource in any knowledge organisation: deep, uninterrupted thinking time. Every hour reclaimed from a status-update meeting is an hour a developer spends writing better code, an hour a strategist spends thinking more clearly, or an hour a manager spends actually coaching their team rather than narrating a slide deck.

The 71% of meetings that senior managers already describe as unproductive are not producing connection. They are producing exhaustion. The Async-First Manifesto simply makes explicit what most people already know: the default was wrong, documents are underrated, and the organisations that figure this out first will compound that advantage year over year.

Start with your meeting audit this week. Block two hours. Export your calendar. Ask the uncomfortable question for every recurring block. You will almost certainly find that the majority of your meeting time is ripe for replacement — and that the documents you write in their place will outlast any call recording by years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an async-first workplace?++

An async-first workplace defaults to written or recorded communication — documents, video logs, shared briefs — and reserves live meetings only for decisions requiring real-time interaction like conflict resolution or sensitive negotiations.

Which meetings can be replaced with documents?++

Status updates, progress reports, project kick-offs, onboarding walkthroughs, and informational broadcasts can almost always be replaced with structured written documents, saving significant time and cost.

How much do unnecessary meetings actually cost companies?++

US companies waste an estimated $37 billion annually on unnecessary meetings. A single 10-person senior-staff meeting costs $1,500–$3,000 per hour when loaded salaries are factored in.

How long does it take to build an async-first culture?++

Companies that track meeting costs and actively replace low-value meetings typically reduce meeting time by 30% within three months. Full cultural adoption usually takes six to twelve months with consistent reinforcement.
